## 1. Scope and approved direction

This annex completes KR-04/05 at logical kernel-document level. It extends control coordination; a failed control withholds dependent operations while an independently authorised notification duty survives. Recovery restores demonstrably current governed state before affected operation. The source principles/architecture remain governing; standards support design and do not supply organisational powers.

Current baseline: AOS-00/Map v0.18; AOS-01–05 v0.6; Core/Shared v0.4; Exchange Register v0.3; Control Coordination v0.2. Six directions DEC-073–078 are approved; exact fields, requirements and profiles remain proposed. Earlier directions DEC-062–072 retain standing.

## 2. Responsibilities and continuity profiles

| Component | Authoritative responsibility | Required reciprocal evidence |

| AOS-05 | Route operation/dependency assessment, isolation/restore, runtime validation and enforced scoped release | Authority/use/configuration/state evidence from 01–04, human acceptance where required. |

Each organisation/function needs an approved continuity profile before use, including the DEC-104 class-specific technical-failure and implicated-person interference assessment, authorised appropriately independent alternate recipient/release controls and owned inadequate-route disposition: reporting purposes/triggers; separately governed permissions/credentials/recipient-verification sources; minimal payload and custody; route dependencies and failure/compromise assumptions; route-change powers; functional tracking owner/recipient/cover/escalation; acknowledgement and escalation times; approved human fallback and resident communication; source scope and completeness/currentness acceptance criteria; restore isolation; required validators and empowered releasers; bounded automatic eligibility; recovery objectives with units and evidence; planned exercises and review. Unset fields block reliance on that profile. Functional roles are specified here; actual people, times and services are not appointed or invented.

“Independent” is assessed against the declared failure scenario, including identity, permission, connectivity, provider, keys, monitoring, custody and human availability. A different endpoint with the same failing dependency is insufficient. Route permission or disabling changes need the approved governance/change record and surviving reporting/exception duty. No single universal topology or always-working route is promised.

### DEC-104 — protected reporting under failure and implicated-person interference

Each mandatory protected-finding category shall have an approved reporting arrangement assessed against both technical failure and interference by implicated people. The profile shall identify the intended recipient, possible conflicts and an appropriately independent authorised alternative; an implicated person shall not be the sole gatekeeper deciding whether the required finding reaches its authorised audience.

Authority/use permissions, minimum report content, recipient verification, custody, route/change dependencies, tracking ownership, acceptance, cover and escalation are established in advance. An external route is included where justified and lawfully authorised; external reporting is not a universal default and a finding creates no extra disclosure power. Planned exercises address failed infrastructure, unavailable/implicated recipients, apparent alternatives controlled by the same implicated gatekeeper and attempted suppression. Missing adequate arrangements remain an explicit owned gap, prevent claims of operational protected-reporting assurance and withhold affected dependent reliance under existing gates; they do not create false delivery, ownership transfer or blanket shutdown of separately authorised service. Original tracking duty persists until actual scoped acceptance.

The organisation’s profile binds each required finding class to normal/alternate authorised audiences, conflict criteria/evidence and accountable assessment, disclosure basis/minimal payload, independently verifiable recipients/permissions, credentials/custody and route/reconfiguration controls, failure/compromise assumptions, tracking/cover/escalation, human fallback, evidence and review triggers. Test both infrastructure/common-dependency independence and organisational release/recipient/custody control; neither implies the other. No administrator, executive sign-off or report-validation step may silently remove a required duty. The declared threat/governance scope and residual limitations must be visible: this is not an unlimited guarantee against every holder of legal/constitutional change powers. Inadequacy returns to an empowered accountable reviewer; the reporting agent cannot invent a recipient or grant itself onward-disclosure authority.

### DEC-105 — declared technical control limits and observed assurance

Operational reliance on implemented technical controls requires explicit scope, limits and failure/recovery behaviour supported by accepted observed evidence. The evidence must cover all enabled action paths within the declared control/threat scope, independently of the acting agent; permission-change enforcement points and timing uncertainty; shared cumulative bounds under concurrency; conservative treatment of unknown effects/reservations; and recovery that applies current restrictions without reviving withdrawn authority or repeating completed effects.

The approved control profile fixes exact functions/paths/dependencies/versions, threat assumptions, effect boundary, currentness and revocation/fencing mechanism, timing/uncertainty bounds and units, accounting scopes/consistency and admission, uncertain exposure/release evidence, recovery source/currentness/completeness, permitted fallback/continuing duty, observability/oracle, assessors and objective acceptance conditions. Timeout, cancellation, worker retirement or failover alone cannot prove no effect, release possibly used capacity or permit consequential repetition. No instantaneous global revocation or recall of an irreversible disclosure is promised. Limits are assessed under actual constraints and powers, not silently accepted as waivers of non-waivable safeguards. Missing/failed evidence blocks affected operational-reliance claims with an owned remedy; separately authorised service/reporting duties remain owned and do not acquire extra permissions.

G3 remains authorisation of a bounded build with fixed scope, explicit dependencies/profiles and required separate review; G4 requires implemented observations, and actual operational approval is separate. An explicitly authorised bounded prototype can investigate the mechanisms before operational assurance exists; this decision itself authorises neither a prototype nor live effects. A failed prototype requires investigation of the implementation, assumptions and alternatives; it is not alone proof of architectural impossibility. Model refusal, a generated explanation, intended configuration, an old checkpoint or retrospective log alone is not evidence that all effect paths were enforced or recovery was current. Actual acceptance must link observations to the exact requirement/local-case/variant/profile and endpoint scope, with limitations and competent assessor disposition.

AOS-05 produces control-boundary/dependency observations and declares the implemented limits with authoritative semantic/control owners. AOS-40 evaluates scoped acceptance and preserves build, implementation-verification and operational approval distinctions. Approved profile/instrument owners determine legitimate settings; accountable human assessors disposition limitations without waiving non-waivable constraints.

## 3. Protected reporting protocol

1. Record the incident/withheld scope, exact operations, known effects/uncertainty and tracking owner; block affected effects through existing CHECK/brake rules. Preserve minimal permitted evidence if normal custody is unavailable, subject to the approved profile.
2. Verify the applicable advance authority and use permission, purpose, validity and audience through independently usable approved evidence. Verify the intended recipient; select minimal permitted payload and approved route/custody. Do not send secret values. Failure of normal controls grants no additional data access. If required evidence is unavailable, withhold unauthorised transmission and retain the owned exception/approved human fallback.
3. Assess recipient/release/custody conflicts against the approved class profile. An implicated person cannot be the sole gatekeeper for the required authorised audience. Use the approved appropriately independent alternative when the normal recipient/route is implicated, unavailable, compromised or suppressing the finding; do not wait for that implicated party’s discretionary approval. Attempt only separately permitted routes within evidenced validity. Record attempt/delivery/receipt/validation separately from explicit human responsibility acceptance. Duplicate/coalesced reporting links every operation and preserves each deadline and remaining duty.
4. The functional tracking owner retains the duty until an empowered human accepts the specified responsibility with scope, identity, time, cover and remaining duties. Delivery receipts and automatic acknowledgements cannot constitute this acceptance. Missed response activates configured cover/escalation. Rejection or expired acceptance returns to an explicitly owned unresolved state; no ownerless transfer.
5. If all technical routes fail, retain an owned unresolved exception and execute the approved human fallback when permitted. Surface the failure to the accountable reviewer through any remaining approved mechanism; never record success without evidence. Arrange appropriate permitted resident updates through agreed channels via the accountable service workflow. Technical report delivery, accepted responsibility and service completion stay distinct.

## 4. Controlled recovery protocol

1. Restore candidate copies/configuration in an isolated environment with normal processing and external effects disabled. Minimal validation processing has its own bounded authority/use permission and protected custody. Record provider/data-flow/location constraints and unresolved compromise.
2. Establish required authoritative scope: authority/restriction/withdrawal from 01; identity/consent/basis/use/lifecycle disposition from 03; approved task/configuration/effect meaning from 04; actual applied configuration, observed effects and account/reservations from 05; common evidence/provenance from 02. A protected backup is an input, not automatically current truth.
3. Prove integrity, currentness and completeness across the restore point and every material subsequent change for the proposed scope. Identify source lineage/revisions, coverage boundaries, independent acceptance evidence and gaps. Timestamp/checksum alone is insufficient; a forged, compromised, inaccessible or incomplete source blocks dependent release. Source mechanisms, cutover fences and completeness proof are commissioned choices, not inferred from replica health.
4. Apply current withdrawals/restrictions and deletion dispositions to every declared applicable restored copy/derived index/memory before normal use. If mandatory disposition cannot yet be proved, retain permitted isolation and an owned remediation obligation. Do not retain a hidden deleted payload in history under the guise of evidence; preserve only separately permitted minimal disposition evidence.
5. Validate exact software/model/tool/schema/control/profile compatibility and accepted configuration against actual applied state. Reconcile completed, partial and unknown attempts/effects and authoritative reservations across providers and periods. Known completed effects are not replayed; uncertain residual exposure remains conservatively reserved until evidence supports release. Neither rollback nor an idempotency claim proves that an external effect did not happen. Continue service and disclosure duties.
6. Submit a scope-specific recovery evidence package to the designated validator/releaser. Record acceptance/rejection/unresolved state, actual powers, applicable human decision, limitations and remaining duties. Compromise, material unresolved consequences or profile conditions require empowered human acceptance. Human acceptance cannot waive missing mandatory current-state/use evidence. Automatic recovery is limited to explicitly preapproved eligible scope with required evidence; otherwise block dependent release.
7. Apply only accepted release scopes, record enforcement/compatible applied state and obtain fresh CHECK and necessary reservations before task continuation. Prove no material dependency for unaffected scopes released separately. No recovered queue is replayed merely because infrastructure is available. Rejected/unresolved scopes retain isolation/brakes and owners. Service duties require their own completion evidence.
