## Approved rule

AOS shall distinguish the validity and effective time of a human decision, the provenance/authentication and evidential standing of its record, the separate approval status of minutes, and current permission to execute an action. It shall assess decision validity and sufficient evidence under the organisation’s applicable law, legal form, constitution/registered rules, powers and approved evidence/reliance policy. It shall not universally require later minutes approval to bring a decision into existence, nor require a second board approval of a separate decision record where those applicable arrangements do not require it.

Minutes approval and authentication are evidence events, not universal substitutes for the actual decision process. An authenticated record or extract may support reliance through an authorised evidence route, but neither its signature nor its label can manufacture a decision, cure missing powers/conditions or make disputed evidence conclusive. Preserve actual decision, record, authentication/approval, effective and execution times separately; no automatic backdating. Contradictory, missing or insufficient evidence withholds affected automated reliance for accountable human resolution without declaring that an otherwise valid legal decision does not exist. Organisation-specific additional assurance gates may be adopted, but must be labelled as AOS/organisation reliance policy rather than general law. Separately authorised urgent/service duties remain owned. Minute-taker drafting and Company Secretary stewardship remain distinct; human decision powers, protected constitutional processes, boardroom AI exclusion, current authority/data-use checks and DEC-088/089 remain unchanged.

## Primary-source research and what it establishes

- [Companies Act 2006, sections 248–249](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/46/part/10/chapter/9/crossheading/records-of-meetings-of-directors): recording/retention and authenticated minutes as evidence with rebuttable presumptions; no general later-minutes-approval decision-creation rule in these provisions.
- [Model articles, companies limited by guarantee, articles 7/8/15](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/model-articles-for-private-companies-limited-by-guarantee/model-articles-for-private-companies-limited-by-guarantee): decision-making and keeping records are distinct; model/default provisions are not evidence of a particular entity’s adopted articles.
- [Woolgar v Newport Capital & Guarantee Ltd [2024] EWHC 1819 (Comm), paragraphs 36–43](https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWHC/Comm/2024/1819.html): signed minutes were assessed against other evidence and were not conclusive proof of the alleged agreement.
- [Burnden Holdings (UK) Ltd v Fielding [2019] EWHC 1566 (Ch), paragraphs 172–173](https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWHC/Ch/2019/1566.html): section 249 presumption and challenger’s burden; the judge explained this further discussion was unnecessary to the preceding conclusion. Neither case establishes a universal subsequent-minutes approval prerequisite.
- [Charity Commission CC48, minutes](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-and-meetings-cc48/charities-and-meetings): guidance contemplates prompt draft circulation to assist carrying out decisions and later minutes approval, subject to governing document; guidance is not case law.
- [Co-operative and Community Benefit Societies Act 2014, section 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/14/section/14): registered rules must address meetings/voting and committee/officer powers; particular rules and applicable provisions must be checked.

Sources reviewed online 29 October 2026; relevant judgment passages retrieved through indexed primary judgment text when direct BAILII opening was blocked. Conclusions are a bounded documentary/legal-source analysis, not independent legal advice for a particular provider.
