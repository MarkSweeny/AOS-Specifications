1. Overall finding

The five drafts form a coherent kernel at the level of responsibilities and governing behaviour. I found no fundamental contradiction in the allocation of authority, data stewardship, permitted use, agent execution and platform enforcement. Their central rules agree: humans retain reserved judgement; agents cannot manufacture permission; authority and permitted use are separate; affected automation is withheld when required controls are missing; uncertain effects require reconciliation; restart cannot revive withdrawn permissions.

The set is not yet a fully reconciled, build-ready kernel. Ten findings below identify incomplete exchanges, unresolved coordination rules, one important evidence-sequencing ambiguity, and document maintenance defects. Several gaps were already declared in the individual drafts; this review makes their cross-component consequences and closure responsibilities explicit. It does not treat deliberately open implementation choices as contradictory decisions.

Recommended next step: reconcile these findings across the five drafts and shared contracts before moving on to the next major drafting tranche. Existing approved directions do not need to be asked again merely to perform that reconciliation. Concrete organisational powers, thresholds, flows and commissioning choices still require their own appropriate approval.

4. Findings and recommended corrections

Priority high means resolve before building or claiming conformance for the affected function. Medium means reconcile before accepting the set as a common document baseline. Low means document presentation repair. These are proposed closure assignments to components; they do not appoint operational people.

KR-01 — Reciprocal exchange definitions are incomplete

High — integration gap. The five §6 catalogues describe compatible exchanges, but do not yet provide a single complete register linking each local CON to its definitive shared definition, producer, every consumer, exact revision and acceptance obligations. Their 33 CON entries are overlapping views, not 33 independently owned interfaces. REC-D and REC-X still lack definitive IF identifiers. Consent changes, identity assurance, agent lifecycle, health/incident, recovery and release exchanges remain mainly descriptive.

Evidence: all five §6 catalogues; Core Records §§2, 5 and 6; Shared Contracts §§4–5 and P01–P04/P09. Existing seven IF identifiers must be preserved.

Consequence: separately built components could accept different field meanings, statuses, acknowledgements or incompatible versions while each appears locally compliant.

Correction: AOS-02 coordinates one shared register; AOS-01/03/04/05 own their respective semantics and reciprocal acceptance. Fix mandatory/conditional fields, units, unknown values, state transitions, rejection, retries and compatibility rules. Distinguish agent lifecycle, task state, execution attempt, observed effect and human case disposition throughout. Allocate missing identifiers through the controlled catalogue rather than inventing local replacements.

KR-02 — There is no completed rule for a current control snapshot at the effect boundary

High — acknowledged coordination gap. All five demand current controls, but freshness, invalidation delivery, in-flight races and mixed-version activation remain open. An approved policy, a current identity, a valid REC-A, a valid REC-D, an active agent and an operating platform are distinct states; their favourable combination is not yet defined precisely enough to govern one effect.

Evidence: AOS-01 REQ-011 and §§5–6; AOS-03 §§5–6; AOS-04 REQ-006–008/025/029; AOS-05 §§6 and 13.2; Core Records P03 and §4.3; Shared Contracts §4.6 explicitly leaves distribution barriers and mixed versions open.

Consequence: a queued operation could use a partly activated policy or a stale consent/restriction while individual services report success.

Correction: AOS-01/03/04/05 jointly define the action-bound checkpoint profile, acceptable current-state evidence, invalidation ordering, partial activation and irreversible-action handling. Add a combined admission table covering mismatches, unavailable controls and changes after checking. Specify residual race limits rather than promising instantaneous global revocation.

KR-03 — The correction notification map omits an important consumer

High — concrete catalogue omission. AOS-02 CON-003 explicitly lists AOS-01/03/04/13/21–25 as correction/lineage consumers but omits AOS-05. Its §10.2 also names a narrower consumer set. AOS-05 nevertheless needs current restrictions and corrected evidence when enforcing, restoring or replaying affected work. This is an incomplete map, not a requirement allowing the platform to ignore changes.

Evidence: AOS-02 §6.1 CON-003 and §10.2; AOS-03 CON-004/REQ-035; AOS-04 REQ-029; AOS-05 §§6, 10.4 and 11.

Correction: add AOS-05 where it has material dependencies, and define how source corrections, identity/representation changes, consent withdrawal, authority changes and deletion/restriction dispositions reach affected consumers. Name the impact assessment owner and distinguish event receipt from actual application. Cover delayed, duplicated and out-of-order changes, including pending queues and restored copies.

KR-04 — Protected reporting during control failure is an obligation without a completed route

High — continuity gap. The drafts consistently require protected findings and outstanding duties to survive agent stoppage or ordinary administrative suppression. They also prohibit sending through an unpermitted route. An independently available, properly authorised reporting and duty-transfer route has not yet been concretely defined for loss or compromise of shared identity, permission, record or delivery dependencies.

Evidence: AOS-01 REQ-027–028; AOS-03 REQ-027–029 and §11; AOS-04 REQ-016–017 and §11; AOS-05 REQ-017–019 and §§10–13; Core Records §5.2 requires an approved metadata-access bootstrap profile.

Consequence: “stop the unsafe action” can work while the required finding never reaches an empowered human. Recording an attempted report does not fulfil its audience obligation.

Correction: AOS-05 coordinates with AOS-01/03/04 and the AOS-13/20/25/30 owners to specify minimal permitted reporting content, independently governed credentials/custody, routes, recipients, acknowledgement, cover and common-failure behaviour. If no lawful usable route exists, retain an explicitly owned unresolved duty and activate the approved human fallback. Do not resolve this gap with a blanket privacy or authority bypass.

KR-05 — Recovery requires current restrictions, but their recovery source and reconciliation proof are incomplete

High — recovery gap. The drafts agree that restored data, queues and grants cannot be used until current restrictions and unknown effects are reconciled. They do not yet fix the authoritative recovery inputs or sufficient proof when the usual current-state service is itself lost or compromised.

Evidence: AOS-01 REQ-029/036 and §11; AOS-02 §§11–13; AOS-03 REQ-030–032/036–037; AOS-04 §10.3; AOS-05 CON-006/007 and §10.4; Shared Contracts P03/P08/P09.

Consequence: an otherwise successful restore can reintroduce deleted material, expired permissions or an uncertain already-dispatched operation. Withholding unknown scope is coherent, but it does not supply a recovery procedure.

Correction: AOS-05 with AOS-01/02/03/04 specifies authoritative restriction/disposition sources, restoration isolation, freshness/version reconciliation, accepted evidence and empowered release of recovered scope. Include provider failover and rollback, outstanding duties, deletion propagation and possible external effects. If currentness cannot be established, affected use stays withheld.

KR-06 — Cumulative-effect limits lack a complete cross-agent enforcement arrangement

High — enforcement gap. DEC-056 and AOS-04 REQ-045 require bounds across linked tasks, agents and periods. AOS-05 requires corresponding measures and limits. The shared accounting scope, enforcement owner and behaviour under simultaneous workers or unavailable accounting are not yet settled.

Evidence: AOS-04 REQ-021/039/045, §§10.2 and 13 and VER-014/025/029; AOS-05 §13.2; AOS-01 ISS-013.

Consequence: individually permitted workers can jointly exceed a contact, commitment or resource bound, especially during retries, cancellation or failover.

Correction: AOS-04/05 jointly define linked-work identity, effect units and period, authoritative accounting, how parallel admission prevents excess, partial/unknown-effect treatment and the approved brake/review route. AOS-01/03 approve applicable authority/use constraints; AOS-02 preserves evidence. Exact values remain commissioning choices. Do not equate staying within a numeric budget with ethical acceptability.

KR-07 — Permission evidence and later enforcement evidence need clearer sequencing

Medium — wording ambiguity, not an established policy contradiction. AOS-03 REQ-040 says a data-use determination retains “actual enforcement evidence”. Core REC-D §5.1 defines the evaluation result, which must exist before dependent use; REC-X §6.1 records the final check and actual effect. The current wording leaves unclear whether enforcement proof is required before issuing the determination or linked after use.

Consequence: a literal implementation could create a circular prerequisite or revise a historical determination in a way that obscures what was originally decided.

Correction: AOS-03 with AOS-02/04/05 distinguishes contemporaneous evaluation evidence from subsequent linked checkpoint/enforcement/effect evidence. Permit or refusal must remain reconstructable at its original revision; later execution evidence identifies that exact determination. Define evidence for withheld operations as well as completed ones, and prohibit a pending evidence link being presented as proof of enforcement.

KR-08 — Assurance provenance is richer in the components than in the detailed field contract

Medium — schema completeness gap. AOS-02 §3.1 and REQ-040–043 require origin to be distinct from supporting evidence, validation method/version, validator, accountable role, currentness, scope and limitations. Core REC-E §3.1 can hold quality and provenance references, but does not yet explicitly map that complete chain to fields or a required companion assurance record. AOS-03 disclosure and AOS-04/05 audit depend on it.

Correction: AOS-02 extends the single shared definition or defines its linked assurance record, with exact requirement-to-field mappings. Carry assurance references and limitations through transformations and authorised projections. A generic source reference or a confidence number must not silently stand in for the approved assurance chain.

KR-09 — The set needs a common decision baseline and accurate contract/status labels

Medium — document reconciliation gap. The five v0.2 drafts cite successively newer architecture/map baselines: .11/.8, .12/.9, .13/.11, .14/.13 and .15/.15. Those accurately describe their drafting history, but do not establish that earlier components have been checked against every later applicable decision. AOS-01 ISS-011 still uses a broad “Decision required” label despite DEC-051 settling the general welfare-protocol direction; detailed board instruments remain open. Its ISS-013 needs DEC-056 alignment. AOS-02 CON-004 still says AOS-03 is “to co-draft”. AOS-01 §6.1 leaves other definitive IF allocation broadly open although Shared Contracts §4.6 already preserves AOS-01-IF-002 for policy amendment/activation.

Correction: revalidate against AOS-00 .15/DEC-001–061 and Map .15, preserving historical origins. Explicitly map CON-002 to the existing policy-activation interface and relevant companion views. Separate approved direction from unresolved organisational detail in issue labels. Complete reciprocal decision/source/REQ/contract/VER traceability; do not merely change version labels or reopen approved general questions.

KR-10 — Four appended register blocks are broken Markdown table continuations

Low — presentation defect. A blank line separates AOS-04 REQ-043–046 and VER-027–030, and AOS-05 REQ-047–050 and VER-035–038, from their preceding tables without a fresh header/separator. These rows have valid identifiers but will not consistently render as part of a table.

Correction: remove the intervening blank lines or introduce complete continuation headers. The five specifications were not edited by this review; this repair belongs in the reconciliation pass.

Three apparent tensions are resolved by the existing boundary rules. AOS-01's human power to suspend a grant does not prohibit AOS-05's separately preauthorised technical isolation. AOS-04's material-change acceptance rule does not prohibit failover to an already accepted, still eligible exact configuration under DEC-061. Evidence preservation does not prohibit governed deletion: permitted lifecycle and minimal disposition records must coexist, without hidden content copies. These should be tested together, but are not conflicting architectural directions.

A finding closes only when the affected producers and consumers agree on the same controlled definition and expected failure behaviour. Merely adding another local requirement or marking a planned case as present does not close an integration gap.
